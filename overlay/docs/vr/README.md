# Original-game VR integration

`scripts/vr/Package.ps1 [-Version <v>] [-NoBuild]` builds the player release: `dist/RatchetVR-<v>/` and its zip, holding `RatchetVR.exe` and `RatchetVR Sandbox.exe` (the launcher, `crates/rc-engine/src/bin/ratchet-vr.rs`), `rerac.exe` (built with `--features vr`, never `target-cpu=native`), `rerac-extract.exe`, `assets/shaders`, README/LICENSE/notices. The folder is checked against that exact file list. On first launch the launcher extracts the player's `.iso` (dropped onto it, alone in its folder, or picked in a file dialog) into `data\` with `--ntsc-only`, then starts `rerac.exe --data-dir data` with Run.ps1's VR environment (`normal`; the Sandbox copy runs `debug`) and saves under `saves\<mode>\`. Extractor and runtime exit codes (docs/plan/launcher_contract.md) become plain-language message boxes.

The default launch is now `normal`: native boot/logos, Press Start and the main menu, followed by ordinary New Game/Load Game progression. Inventory, initial ammunition, planets and story scenes use their original state; VR debug grants are disabled. `-Mode debug` names the former direct-Kerwan launch with every inventory item, full initial reserves and all planets unlocked. `-Mode debug-gold` adds the native ten gold upgrades to that same debug setup. `-Scene novalis` selects the previous level only in debug modes; calibration remains explicit. Each launch mode has a separate native memory card under `work/vr/saves/<mode>`.


`vr/game.rs` installs OpenXR cameras on the original Bevy world. Each eye has the original world materials and a sky view with the same runtime pose/projection. Both use the existing render passes, including shadows and display effects. The original mono game frame remains available for the window, menu snapshots and menus shown inside the headset.

`vr/game_input.rs` maps OpenXR locomotion and menu actions into `input_map::PadFrame`. The original fixed tick consumes those libpad2 bytes. Hero physics, animations, enemy updates, pickups, inventory, pause and respawn remain the original game systems. A maps immediately to Cross for jump/double jump; holding the left index trigger (native R1) and pressing A on the ground requests the equipped pack's high/long jump, with native side/back flips taking priority for a timed directional flick. Right-stick click sends Triangle in gameplay and menus: native context interactions take priority; otherwise hold the right-stick click, move the right hand toward an icon, then release to confirm quick select. The left joystick keeps moving Ratchet during selection (the right hand picks). B/X retain Square/Circle in menus, with B also exiting the Hologuise; in play X holds L2 and B R2 (Square/Circle on turrets and ships). In menus the left grip sends L1 and the left trigger R1. Left stick click sends Select and Y sends Start; during scenes/movies a fresh Y press queues native skip, including the opening fade/18-tick scene window. Start is suppressed until release on return to gameplay. F8 recenters. The right grip holds the wrench and releases a throw. The left trigger retains native R1 movement-state rules, including crouch; physical headset height is not driven by the crouch animation. There is no teleport binding. For any selected gun/utility gadget, the right trigger sends native Circle. Throwing gloves instead prepare and physically release their original object. Controller loss clears XR input. While XR input is active, ordinary gamepad input is ignored to avoid SteamVR virtual-gamepad duplicates.

The game remains at 60 Hz. The desktop 60 fps limiter is disabled in VR and OpenXR paces headset rendering. The original shared culling/LOD/lighting systems explicitly use the main camera instead of accidentally choosing an eye, sky or menu camera. Its culling envelope is expanded for head tracking; the single-view authored PS2 occlusion mask is disabled for VR.

Head tracking is relative to a first-person anchor above the original hero position (1.1 on foot, Ratchet's own eye height rather than the native first-person camera's 1.6; blending at 2 units/s to 0.45 in native swim groups). No animated bone or follow-camera rotation drives this anchor. Initial calibration waits for headset focus and accommodates seated/standing height. Left-stick input is converted from headset heading into the original simulation camera's frame. Right stick turns by 30 degrees with hysteresis, pivoting about the current headset position. Menus retain their ordinary stick directions and authored camera views. World scale remains configurable, defaulting to one game unit per metre.

Horizontal room motion is consumed once: `align_body` passes successive physical head deltas to `Play::roomscale_move`, which calls the original hero capsule resolver in short steps against world and moby collision. It preserves stick/jump velocity and does not advance simulation or platform carry a second time. The hero moby, collision grid and renderer are synchronized before gameplay input. The tracking origin subtracts the current horizontal HMD position, keeping the eyes above the resolved capsule; there is no accumulated snap-turn pivot offset. Focus/menu transitions rebase tracking, and abrupt tracking jumps are not applied as character movement. Vertical head movement remains relative to calibrated eye height; crouch is not synthesized.

`vr/embodied.rs` samples valid controller grip poses. Original brown Ratchet hand meshes and textures follow both hands; the original animated body is now visible in the first-person world. Original weapon/gadget meshes/materials/animations are presented at the right hand through `MobyAttach::tracked_items`. The body retains its native animation while controller hand/item presentation remains tracked.

The wrench is 20% larger and extends along controller forward by up to another 15% with tracked swing speed, preserving the palm anchor. The tracked wrench transform compensates the full attachment-list-0 grip frame: model rotation is the palm frame rotated -90 degrees about controller-local X times the inverse grip frame (shaft along controller forward), and the authored grip translation (class scale / 1024) is subtracted from the model origin. Melee, held rendering and launch use this same conversion; the catch target uses the held animation rather than the spinning flight pose. Runtime alignment telemetry reports actual eye-to-body horizontal separation and held grip-to-palm separation.

Physical swings sweep the original wrench-head joint through the existing collision scene before `GameTick`, with native 0.17-unit extension and 0.35-unit head radius, deliver normal wrench hit records, and reuse the hit sound. Enemy hits require tip speed >=4 m/s, translating hand speed >=1.8 m/s and at least 10 cm hand travel from rest. Crates require >=5 m/s tip speed, >=2.2 m/s hand speed and >=12 cm travel. Rest below 0.65 m/s rearms the stroke origin; wrist-only speed cannot bypass these gates. A downward hand stroke of at least 10 cm at 1.5 m/s in the air requests native state 0x14, subject to its original airborne state, animation, time and clearance gates. Native Hyper Strike owns the windup, accelerated fall, damage 2 and landing shockwave; ordinary damage-1 gesture sweeps are suspended during that state. A target cooldown limits repeated impacts; world and moby obstruction checks prevent striking through walls. Only tracking-space motion counts: locomotion and snap turns cannot generate a swing. A release above 1 m/s launches the original hand item with its native outgoing/returning flight, damage, bounce, spin and pickup logic. The optional `HeroItems::tracked_hand` attachment changes its in-hand pose and catch point; when None, the original non-VR animated attachment runs unchanged.

Slow grip releases stow the wrench. Lost tracking, lost focus, menus, recenter and snap turns clear gesture history; releases expire rather than being queued into a later equipment swap. A fresh grip requests the wrench once through the normal inventory slot, preserving subsequent native quick-select choices until the next grip press. Quick select anchors the left controller position and HMD view plane at Y press. Hand displacement in that plane selects a sector beyond 6 cm, with a 3.5 cm inner dead zone. Tracking loss/recenter rebases the anchor; snap turning is suspended while Y is held. The gesture updates only menu analog-copy fields between GameTick and MenuFrameSet, so it cannot drive hero locomotion. Y retains native contextual interaction priority. HUD and menus remain panels. Physical alignment, gesture thresholds and comfort need headset feedback. Existing unported upstream behavior is inherited.

`vr/bomb_glove.rs` adds the right-trigger Bomb Glove gesture. Each press prepares one native class-121 moby if the glove is owned and ammo is available. While held, `NO_UPDATE` and disabled collision suspend its autonomous update and fuse; the native dynamic renderer shows the original model, with `Play::tracked_bomb_pose` following the hand even between simulation ticks. This direct gesture access coexists with the wrench without changing the saved equipment selection.

A tracked release above 1 m/s starts native `bomb::release` with hand-derived velocity (gain 3, capped at 18 m/s, converted to original game units per tick) plus the hero's current displacement. The native scheduler then owns flight, collisions, water, fuse, explosion, damage, audio and effects. The original use-ammo path consumes one bomb at launch; both ammo mirrors are updated before GameTick so the normal save/statistics synchronization retains the decrement. Slow release, lost tracking/focus, menus, death or obstruction deletes only the prepared bomb and consumes no ammo. A bomb in hand suppresses wrench melee; a wrench already in flight continues normally.

The original Bomb Glove (class 192) replaces the brown right-hand mesh while equipped or preparing a bomb. Its stable authored pose is mounted by inverse palm-list-0 translation/rotation, with the fingers along controller forward and the palm up. The same palm frame determines the existing held-bomb offset. Presentation can draw both the tracked glove and an already flying wrench without changing the native hand slot or bomb simulation. The brown left hand remains; the brown right hand uses its original gripping pose with handheld weapons and its free palm placement when empty, and all hand meshes hide when tracking is unavailable.

The default hands use original Ratchet class-0 glove/cuff triangles weighted to joints 74..91 (left) and 54..71 (right), preserving UVs, textures and native skinning. Two presentation copies retain the native layered pose and palm frames at joints 76/56; left fingers extend +Y and right fingers -Y in their original skeletons. The procedural sphere hands are removed. The Bomb Glove substitutes only the right hand.

`hero/tracked.rs` and `vr/traversal.rs` add optional inputs to the native movement states. The left index trigger sends native R1 with independent press/release hysteresis. Airborne R1 and chained flips retain native rules. Native pack ownership, wall checks and launch curves remain. A reaches Cross immediately, and held A retains height/glide. The right trigger is no longer consumed by pack/flip combinations and keeps weapon use independent. Head orientation never follows animated flips or camera shake.

Native ledge probes still validate level/moby edges and top clearance. A tracked grip within the edge's reach window permits the grab. Each gripping hand stays at its captured world anchor. Tracking-space hand displacement pulls the native body oppositely along the ledge tangent, through the original top/wall/clearance probes and hang/capsule physics. Left-stick translation is neutralized both in XR input and native held-ledge physics, including d-pad fallback. A still invokes its native climb action. Releasing both hands drops with the native regrab lockout. A and the shove climb even below a higher ledge or rung: the native climb jump then catches it, lands on an open top, or regrabs the ledge. Native platform handling continues to carry the ledge.

Palm-normal drag allows edge-on recovery; native surface buoyancy, underwater oxygen, states and collision remain. An upward push of water requests a native dive. The swimmer's eye-height blend puts controller hands near the actual water surface.

A continuous downward foot sweep catches walkable solid faces crossed during falling/integration/capsule correction before the ordinary native ground probe. Liquid surfaces retain their native rules. Room motion preserves the previous safe simulation-history point for capsule rollback. This addresses two concrete fall-through mechanisms; full level behavior still needs physical play feedback.

## Weapon grip, Morph-o-Ray and physical Walloper correction

The original held meshes were inspected read-only with their native skinning and joint lists (`work/vr/read-weapon-geometry.rs`, `weapon-geometry.json/png` in the local build). The old mounting aligned an animated muzzle list frame at an arbitrary reach. That is inappropriate for rear grips, and some joint lists are rotated or are on a sight rather than the barrel. Calibrated grip points now determine placement for Devastator, Suck Cannon, Blaster, Morph-o-Ray, Pyrocitor, R.Y.N.O., Visibomb and Walloper. The local weapon frame is stable: X up, -Y forward, Z lateral. The real animated muzzle is transformed after grip placement, independently. The Visibomb's sight list is replaced by its actual front opening for tracked launch. Grip centres are geometric approximations and remain subject to physical feedback.

The Morph-o-Ray previously read the native body's `moby_rows` even while other gun updates received the tracked aim. It now reads controller forward directly, searches only the tracked cone, drops an out-of-cone lock immediately and seeds the beam centreline from current aim without clearing pulse/strand animation. Tracked beam length uses 3-D distance and its strand frame remains valid when aiming vertically. Desktop behavior keeps the original path.

`vr/walloper.rs` detects a right-hand punch from the hand's motion relative to the head (walking or leaning does not count), thrown within about 55° of the player's facing and away from the spine (an arm folding back, a sideways swing or a backward step never punches), and `walloper::tracked_fire` enters native state 0x20, subject to the original grounded/ready activation rules. The physical gesture uses the original attack windup, 18-unit/s target speed, speed ramp, dash and hit window, rather than a separate instant strike or custom launch. The lunge goes where the player faces, turned at most 25° toward the punch (or, for a trigger attack, the controller's aim); that yaw drives the movement, Ratchet's facing during the lunge and the original three radius-0.8 attack spheres, and the windup keeps only the motion along it. In tracked mode only planar punch motion is injected; original gravity handles vertical motion. Damage 3, native eligibility, push, sounds, sparkles and arcs are retained. Desktop lunge physics is unchanged. Gesture initiation remains 7 cm / 1.45 m/s, with quiet rearm; trigger takes priority.

The original body is visible for inspection; the layer-61 body exclusion is removed. Body animation remains native. The Swingshot (class 208) alone receives a +90-degree controller-local Z roll, taking the housing from the right side to the top of the wrist while keeping the grip anchor and forward axis fixed. Its tracked attachment and hook/rope output derive from the same corrected model frame.

The camera transports the tracking frame's up axis with the native body on magnetic tracks. Eye height, hands, snap turns and room movement use this floor frame; native gravity/capsule physics remains authoritative. Jump/flip animation cannot tilt the view. Physical transitions, ledge anchors on moving platforms and thresholds remain subject to headset feedback.

Both-controller OpenXR haptics are bound for Touch, Index and Microsoft profiles. `vr/haptics.rs` observes committed native ammo-use statistics, actual Blaster spawns, per-missile RYNO counters, Suck Cannon capture/fire, native beam/flame states and actual gadget transitions. Wrench impact feedback remains. Short timed patterns are arbitrated by priority; accepted damage gives both hands a full-strength 135 ms pulse plus a softer tail, overriding weapon cues. Focus/menu loss clears pending feedback and statistics are rebased across load/respawn. Weapon/gadget cues require tracked hands; priority damage can reach an active controller during a brief optical occlusion. Empty fire and cancelled throws do not produce firing recoil.
