# Original-game VR integration

Novalis runs through `main.rs`, `level_switch::load_bundle`, and the original ReRAC plugin chain. The terrain-only application is no longer the gameplay entry point. Calibration is an explicit diagnostic mode.

`vr/game.rs` installs OpenXR cameras on the original Bevy world. Each eye has the original world materials and a sky view with the same runtime pose/projection. Both use the existing render passes, including shadows and display effects. The original mono game frame remains available for the window, menu snapshots and menus shown inside the headset.

`vr/game_input.rs` maps OpenXR locomotion and menu actions into `input_map::PadFrame`. The original fixed tick consumes those libpad2 bytes. Hero physics, animations, enemy updates, pickups, inventory, pause and respawn remain the original game systems. A maps to Cross for jump/double jump. Y sends Triangle in gameplay and menus: native context interactions take priority; otherwise hold Y, move the left hand toward an icon, then release to confirm quick select. The left joystick is ignored during selection. B/X retain Square/Circle only in menus. Left/right stick clicks send Select/Start; F8 recenters. The right grip holds the wrench and releases a throw. There are no XR crouch or teleport bindings. When the Blaster is selected, the right trigger sends its native Circle fire action. Controller loss clears XR input. While XR input is active, ordinary gamepad input is ignored to avoid SteamVR virtual-gamepad duplicates.

The game remains at 60 Hz. The desktop 60 fps limiter is disabled in VR and OpenXR paces headset rendering. The original shared culling/LOD/lighting systems explicitly use the main camera instead of accidentally choosing an eye, sky or menu camera. Its culling envelope is expanded for head tracking; the single-view authored PS2 occlusion mask is disabled for VR.

Head tracking is relative to a stable first-person anchor 1.6 game units above the original hero position, the game's own Ratchet first-person eye height. No animated bone or follow-camera rotation drives this anchor. Initial calibration waits for headset focus and accommodates seated/standing height. Left-stick input is converted from headset heading into the original simulation camera's frame. Right stick turns by 30 degrees with hysteresis, pivoting about the current headset position. Menus retain their ordinary stick directions and authored camera views. World scale remains configurable, defaulting to one game unit per metre.

Horizontal room motion is consumed once: `align_body` passes successive physical head deltas to `Play::roomscale_move`, which calls the original hero capsule resolver in short steps against world and moby collision. It preserves stick/jump velocity and does not advance simulation or platform carry a second time. The hero moby, collision grid and renderer are synchronized before gameplay input. The tracking origin subtracts the current horizontal HMD position, keeping the eyes above the resolved capsule; there is no accumulated snap-turn pivot offset. Focus/menu transitions rebase tracking, and abrupt tracking jumps are not applied as character movement. Vertical head movement remains relative to calibrated eye height; crouch is not synthesized.

`vr/embodied.rs` samples valid controller grip poses. Original brown Ratchet hand meshes and textures follow both hands; the original animated body is excluded from the first-person render layers. Original wrench and Blaster meshes/materials/animations are presented at the right hand through `MobyAttach::tracked_items`. This is a hands-only presentation, without full-body IK.

The tracked wrench transform compensates the full attachment-list-0 grip frame: model rotation is the palm frame rotated -90 degrees about controller-local X times the inverse grip frame (shaft along controller forward), and the authored grip translation (class scale / 1024) is subtracted from the model origin. Melee, held rendering and launch use this same conversion; the catch target uses the held animation rather than the spinning flight pose. Runtime alignment telemetry reports actual eye-to-body horizontal separation and held grip-to-palm separation.

Physical swings sweep the original wrench-head joint through the existing collision scene before `GameTick`, deliver normal wrench hit records, and reuse the hit sound. A target cooldown limits repeated impacts; world and moby obstruction checks prevent striking through walls. Only tracking-space motion counts: locomotion and snap turns cannot generate a swing. A release above 1 m/s launches the original hand item with its native outgoing/returning flight, damage, bounce, spin and pickup logic. The optional `HeroItems::tracked_hand` attachment changes its in-hand pose and catch point; when None, the original non-VR animated attachment runs unchanged.

Slow grip releases stow the wrench. Lost tracking, lost focus, menus, recenter and snap turns clear gesture history; releases expire rather than being queued into a later equipment swap. A fresh grip requests the wrench once through the normal inventory slot, preserving subsequent native quick-select choices until the next grip press. Quick select anchors the left controller position and HMD view plane at Y press. Hand displacement in that plane selects a sector beyond 6 cm, with a 3.5 cm inner dead zone. Tracking loss/recenter rebases the anchor; snap turning is suspended while Y is held. The gesture updates only menu analog-copy fields between GameTick and MenuFrameSet, so it cannot drive hero locomotion. Y retains native contextual interaction priority. HUD and menus remain panels. Physical alignment, gesture thresholds and comfort need headset feedback. Existing unported upstream behavior is inherited.

`vr/bomb_glove.rs` adds the right-trigger Bomb Glove gesture. Each press prepares one native class-121 moby if the glove is owned and ammo is available. While held, `NO_UPDATE` and disabled collision suspend its autonomous update and fuse; the native dynamic renderer shows the original model, with `Play::tracked_bomb_pose` following the hand even between simulation ticks. This direct gesture access coexists with the wrench without changing the saved equipment selection.

A tracked release above 1 m/s starts native `bomb::release` with hand-derived velocity (gain 3, capped at 18 m/s, converted to original game units per tick) plus the hero's current displacement. The native scheduler then owns flight, collisions, water, fuse, explosion, damage, audio and effects. The original use-ammo path consumes one bomb at launch; both ammo mirrors are updated before GameTick so the normal save/statistics synchronization retains the decrement. Slow release, lost tracking/focus, menus, death or obstruction deletes only the prepared bomb and consumes no ammo. A bomb in hand suppresses wrench melee; a wrench already in flight continues normally.

The original Bomb Glove (class 192) replaces the brown right-hand mesh while equipped or preparing a bomb. Its stable authored pose is mounted by inverse palm-list-0 translation/rotation, with the fingers along controller forward and the palm up. The same palm frame determines the existing held-bomb offset. Presentation can draw both the tracked glove and an already flying wrench without changing the native hand slot or bomb simulation. The brown left hand remains; the brown right hand returns when another item is selected, and all hand meshes hide when tracking is unavailable.

The default hands use original Ratchet class-0 triangles wholly weighted to joints 75..90 (left) and 55..70 (right), preserving UVs, textures and native skinning. Two presentation-only copies keep a fixed pose and inverse palm frames at joints 76/56; left fingers extend +Y and right fingers -Y in their original skeletons. The procedural sphere hands are removed. The Bomb Glove substitutes only the right hand.

## Verification

`vr/blaster.rs` grants missing Blaster (15) and Pyrocitor (16) through native GiveItem, including vendor ownership and free quick-select slots. Once per application launch, every owned ammo-using weapon is filled to its native item-record capacity (+0x0e); normal ammo consumption then resumes. Both weapons use their authored muzzle list 0 and local -Y barrel axis. Model +X is mounted up, correcting the Blaster's previous 90-degree left roll without changing its aim. Muzzle reach is 40 cm for the Blaster and 65 cm for the larger Pyrocitor, 3 cm above the controller palm. Trigger hysteresis sends native Circle for the selected ready gun with a valid tracked right hand. Optional tracked muzzle/forward override body/camera aim; native firing, cadence, ammo, impacts, damage, particles and sounds remain. Pyrocitor pilot flame, point light, flame launch/collision sweeps and kept-flame hit spheres use the tracked nozzle/direction. Lost/blocked tracking, hidden hand, death or quick select suspend firing and release the flamethrower loop. Non-VR and desktop-preview aim remain original.

`Run.ps1 -DesktopPreview -SmokeTestSeconds 12` boots the ORIGINAL game and renders two offscreen world views plus both sky views. `ORIGINAL_GAME_SMOKE_PASS` requires rendered frames, active game ticks and Ratchet meshes. This does not verify physical headset output. Logs report the hero position/state and entity/view counts without Windows captures.

Use `-Scene calibration` only for the diagnostic room. Its standalone trigger/teleport math and diagnostic targets are not used for gameplay. CPU loop intervals are not GPU duration; a successful launch does not establish a compositor performance budget or full campaign playthrough.

The monitor world view refreshes once per four headset frames during gameplay to reduce the cost of a third full world render. Menus keep their original full-rate frame. Set `RC_VR_MIRROR_EVERY=1` for a full-rate PC view. This setting does not reduce headset updates or simulation ticks.
